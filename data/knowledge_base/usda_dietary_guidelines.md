# USDA and FDA Dietary Guidelines and Daily Value Reference

## Daily Value (DV) Reference Standards
Daily Values (DVs) are standard reference amounts established by the FDA based on a standard 2,000-calorie daily diet for adults and children 4 years of age and older:
- **Total Fat:** 78g maximum per day (35% of daily calories)
- **Saturated Fat:** 20g maximum per day (<10% of daily calories)
- **Trans Fat:** 0g (should be avoided entirely; artificial trans fats / PHOD are banned by FDA)
- **Cholesterol:** 300mg maximum per day
- **Sodium:** 2,300mg maximum per day (about 1 teaspoon of table salt)
- **Total Carbohydrates:** 275g per day
- **Dietary Fiber:** 28g minimum per day
- **Total Sugars:** No DV established, but Added Sugars limit is 50g per day (<10% of daily calories)
- **Protein:** 50g per day

## Evaluating Nutrient Density (% Daily Value Rule)
- **5% DV or less per serving is LOW:** A food is considered low in a nutrient if it provides 5% or less of the Daily Value per serving. This is ideal for nutrients consumers should limit, such as Saturated Fat, Sodium, and Added Sugars.
- **20% DV or more per serving is HIGH:** A food is considered high or rich in a nutrient if it provides 20% or more of the Daily Value per serving. This is desirable for beneficial nutrients such as Dietary Fiber, Vitamin D, Calcium, Iron, and Potassium.

## Sodium Intake Guidelines
- Chronic high sodium intake (>2,300mg/day) increases risk for hypertension, stroke, and cardiovascular disease.
- Foods with >480mg sodium per serving are considered high sodium for an individual food item.

## Sugar Guidelines
- Naturally occurring sugars (found in whole fruits, milk) come packaged with fiber, vitamins, and minerals.
- Added sugars (table sugar, high fructose corn syrup, fruit juice concentrates) contribute calories without essential nutrients and increase risk for Type 2 diabetes and dental caries.
