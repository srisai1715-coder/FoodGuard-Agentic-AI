# FDA Major Food Allergens and Allergen Labeling Guide

## Overview of Major Food Allergens (The Big 9)
Under the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (FALCPA) and the FASTER Act of 2021, nine major food allergens are identified by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA):
1. **Milk** (including whey, casein, butter, lactose, cheese, milk solids)
2. **Eggs** (including egg whites, egg yolks, albumin, lecithin from egg)
3. **Fish** (such as bass, flounder, cod, salmon, tuna)
4. **Crustacean Shellfish** (such as crab, lobster, shrimp, crayfish)
5. **Tree Nuts** (such as almonds, pecans, walnuts, cashews, pistachios, hazelnuts, macadamia nuts)
6. **Peanuts** (including peanut butter, peanut oil, peanut flour)
7. **Wheat** (including spelt, kamut, farro, semolina, wheat starch)
8. **Soybeans** (including soy protein, edamame, tofu, soy lecithin, soy sauce)
9. **Sesame** (added under the FASTER Act effective January 1, 2023, including tahini, sesame seed oil, sesame flour)

## Allergen Labeling Requirements
- Manufacturers must declare the presence of any Big 9 allergen in plain language either in the ingredient list (e.g., "lecithin (soy)") or in a separate "Contains" statement immediately following or adjacent to the ingredient list (e.g., "Contains: Wheat, Milk, Soy.").
- Specific species must be named for Fish (e.g., cod, salmon), Crustacean Shellfish (e.g., shrimp, crab), and Tree Nuts (e.g., almonds, cashews).

## Cross-Contact & Precautionary Statements
- Precautionary statements like "May contain wheat", "Made on shared equipment with peanuts", or "Processed in a facility that also processes tree nuts" are voluntary advisory statements.
- Advisory statements indicate potential cross-contact during manufacturing but do not substitute for good manufacturing practices (GMP).
- An advisory statement ("May contain") does NOT guarantee the allergen is present, nor does its absence guarantee that cross-contact did not occur.

## Distinction Between Absence and Insufficient Data
- If a product label does NOT list a specific allergen in either the ingredient list or Contains statement, it is classified as "Not Found in Label Ingredients".
- Absence of proof on an imperfect OCR scan or incomplete product database entry must NEVER be interpreted as a guaranteed "Allergen Free" safety certification.
